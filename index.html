RNA-seq Cancer Classification Using TCGA Data

Built an R workflow for kidney renal clear cell carcinoma RNA-seq data, combining differential expression, PCA, and four classifiers to investigate tumor-versus-normal gene expression patterns.

50 + 50Tumor & normal tissue samples selected
4Classification models compared
R / tidymodelsTCGAbiolinkslimmaPCA
Project details
Research question

Can gene expression profiles distinguish kidney clear cell carcinoma tissue from normal kidney tissue? Which expression patterns and clinical variables merit further exploration?

Data acquisition & preprocessing

Retrieved TCGA-KIRC RNA-seq data with TCGAbiolinks, selecting 50 primary tumor and 50 normal tissue samples. Retained protein-coding genes, applied quality preprocessing, gene-length normalization, and low-expression filtering, then prepared expression matrices for statistical analysis and modeling.

Exploratory analysis & feature selection

Performed differential expression analysis with limma, using an FDR threshold of 0.01 and a logFC threshold of 1. Explored gene–gene relationships and associations between expression and clinical variables. Used high-variance gene heatmaps and PCA of the top 1,000 variable genes to inspect tumor and normal sample distributions, and selected 1,000 high-variance genes as classification features.

Classification & tuning

Built logistic regression, radial-basis-function SVM, neural network, and random forest models in tidymodels. Used a stratified training/test split, with zero-variance removal and normalization in the modeling workflow. Applied cross-validation and grid search to tune SVM, neural network, and random forest hyperparameters.

Model comparison & interpretation

Compared classifiers using ROC/AUC, accuracy, F1, sensitivity, specificity, and confusion matrices. Used vip and DALEX to examine feature importance and prediction contributions. In the project's reported data split, neural network and random forest models performed better than logistic regression.

Output & scope

Produced an R analysis workflow, differential-expression and PCA visualizations, classifier comparisons, and feature interpretations. The project focuses on tumor-versus-normal tissue classification in the selected TCGA-KIRC samples. Results are exploratory and require independent-data validation to assess generalization.

Drug discovery · Academic project

Drug Repositioning Workflow for DKD Therapeutics

Developed a workflow combining molecular docking and random forests to prioritize four candidates from 500 approved drugs for diabetic kidney disease research, with a plan for experimental validation.

500Approved drugs screened
4Candidates prioritized for follow-up
PythonRDKitSminaRandom forest
Project details
Research goal

Explore approved-drug repurposing against ASK1 (MAP3K5), identifying candidates for further investigation in an anti-fibrotic research strategy for diabetic kidney disease.

Library preparation & structural analysis

Started with 4,392 approved small-molecule drug records from ChEMBL, applied PAINS filtering, desalting, structure standardization, and physicochemical-property filters, then selected 500 compounds for analysis. Used PCA, fingerprint clustering, and Tanimoto similarity to examine chemical space and structural diversity. Selected ASK1 structure 6OYT, with Selonsertib and Staurosporine as docking references.

Docking evaluation & machine learning

Inspected redocked reference-ligand poses. A small benchmark of three positive reference compounds and seven ZINC decoys produced a docking ROC AUC of 0.857. In the subsequent approved-drug-versus-ZINC screening set, docking scores alone showed limited class separation (AUC approximately 0.28). I therefore added a random forest using molecular weight, LogP, TPSA, hydrogen-bond counts, and docking scores, with a 70/30 split and five-fold stratified cross-validation.

Model results & interpretation

Reported random forest test results were AUC 0.99, accuracy 95.1%, and F1 0.93. At this stage, approved drugs were assigned positive labels and ZINC decoys negative labels. These metrics describe classification within that screening dataset; they are not independent validation of ASK1 inhibitory activity in new compounds.

Candidate prioritization

Combined docking scores below −7 kcal/mol with model prediction scores to select 30 candidates, then examined binding poses, Tanimoto structural similarity, and a simple LogP/TPSA-based estimate of blood–brain barrier penetration. Prioritized Cabotegravir, Topotecan, Deucravacitinib, and Bortezomib for follow-up investigation.

Proposed experimental validation

Proposed ASK1 kinase assays, HK-2 renal tubular cell cytotoxicity testing, ROS measurements, and downstream p-JNK/p-p38 analyses, with a budget and timeline for later diabetic kidney disease mouse studies. These experiments were proposed, not completed; ASK1 inhibition and renal protection remain to be established experimentally.

Sequencing quality & genome alignment · Academic project

NGS Analysis Pipeline (Python)

Built a Python workflow connecting FASTQ quality summaries, BWA-MEM genome alignment, and SAMtools processing to prepare sorted, indexed BAM files from raw sequencing reads.

FASTQQCBWA-MEMBAM / BAI
PythonBiopythonBWA-MEMSAMtools
Project details
Question & my contribution

How can FASTQ quality summaries, reference-genome alignment, and alignment-file preparation be coordinated in a Python workflow? I designed separate functions for quality analysis, alignment, SAM/BAM conversion, sorting, and indexing, then connected them in a sequential processing function.

FASTQ quality summaries

Parsed FASTQ records with Biopython SeqIO, accumulated read and base counts and Phred scores, and calculated the mean quality across all bases. Converted that mean Phred score into an estimated error probability and base-call accuracy, reporting summaries to the terminal and analysis_output.txt. These estimates are derived from quality scores.

Genome alignment & BAM processing

Used subprocess to run BWA and SAMtools. Checked for BWA reference-index files and attempted bwa index when files were missing. Generated SAM alignments with BWA-MEM, then converted to BAM, sorted the alignments, and created BAI indexes. The default example uses the human GRCh38 reference genome.

Execution & outputs

Accepted FASTQ and reference-genome paths as command-line arguments. The default example runs independent alignments and processing for the R1 and R2 FASTQ files. Outputs include a quality report, SAM and BAM files, sorted BAMs, and indexes for downstream analysis or genome-browser inspection.

Analysis scope

The project focuses on NGS read-quality summaries and genome-alignment preprocessing, demonstrating Python file parsing, function design, and coordination of external tools. Gene-expression quantification and differential-expression testing are downstream analysis stages.

Cheminformatics & machine learning · Academic project

Blood–Brain Barrier Penetration Prediction Using Random Forest

Built a Python random forest model to predict blood–brain barrier (BBB) penetration using ECFP4 molecular fingerprints and stratified cross-validation, then explored predictions for melatonin receptor ligands and ALK compounds.

0.906Mean cross-validation ROC AUC reported
5-foldStratified cross-validation
Python / RDKitscikit-learnECFP4Random forest
Project details
Research question & data

Can molecular structure features help predict whether a compound crosses the blood–brain barrier? Used the Week 08 BBB dataset, converting p/n labels into binary classes, and explored predictions on melatonin receptor ligands and an ALK activity dataset. Completed as Assignment 08 for RBIF 110 Cheminformatics.

Molecular preparation & chemical-space exploration

Parsed SMILES with RDKit, excluded unparseable structures, and calculated molecular weight, LogP, TPSA, hydrogen-bond donors/acceptors, and rotatable bonds. Examined property distributions, PCA, and fingerprint similarity to explore chemical-space differences between the BBB data and prediction ligands. Physicochemical properties supported exploration and interpretation; the classifier used molecular fingerprints as inputs.

Model development & evaluation

Encoded molecules as ECFP4/Morgan fingerprints with radius 2 and 1,024 bits. Built a 100-tree random forest classifier and evaluated ROC AUC, accuracy, F1 score, and confusion matrices with five-fold stratified cross-validation. The report recorded mean ROC AUC of 0.906, accuracy of 0.884, and F1 score of 0.928, representing internal cross-validation on the coursework dataset.

Ligand predictions & interpretation

Produced BBB class predictions and scores for melatonin receptor ligands, interpreting individual cases alongside molecular weight and AlogP. Also applied the model to ALK activity data, where the report classified approximately 99.6% of records as BBB-penetrant. This prompted discussion of class distributions, chemical-space differences, and model generalization. Predictions support hypotheses for independent-data or experimental validation.

Proteomics Data Analysis of Human Fetal Gliomas

Reanalyzed label-free quantitative (LFQ) mass spectrometry data from a glioma-related study in R, connecting missing-data handling and protein comparisons with interaction networks and pathway enrichment.

3 + 3Control & WT replicates analyzed
150Proteins prioritized for follow-up analysis
R / BioconductorDEPLFQPPI / ORA
Project details
Dataset & analysis scope

Reanalyzed public proteomics data from Bressan et al. (2021; PXD021270), focusing on Control and WT conditions with three replicates each. After identifying more than 80% missing values in one G34R replicate, the report limited its comparison to Control and WT. This is an academic reanalysis of cell-study data.

Data preparation & quality assessment

Organized LFQ intensities, protein identifiers, and gene annotations from proteinGroups.txt, resolved duplicate names, and created a SummarizedExperiment object. Treated zero intensities as missing, applied KNN imputation (k = 5), and used variance-stabilizing normalization (VSN). Examined the data with PCA, sample correlations, and clustered heatmaps.

Protein comparison & ranking

Compared Control and WT with protein-level t-tests and Benjamini–Hochberg multiple-testing correction. Ranked proteins by adjusted p-value and selected the top 150 for further exploration, using expression heatmaps and a volcano plot to inspect changes. The top 150 form a ranked list, rather than a set necessarily passing a significance threshold.

Interaction networks & pathway exploration

Mapped UniProt identifiers to gene symbols and Entrez IDs, curated known direct interactions from HPRD, and constructed a subnetwork of selected proteins. Combined pathway annotations with Fisher's exact tests for over-representation analysis (ORA) to explore related biological processes. Network edges represent database-annotated interactions.

Output

Produced an R data-preparation workflow, quality and protein-expression visualizations, a ranked list of 150 proteins, an interaction subnetwork, and pathway enrichment tables. Results support follow-up hypotheses, with interpretation accounting for the small replicate count, imputation choices, annotations, and background-gene selection.

Single-cell analysis & quality control · Academic project

Single-Cell Flow Cytometry Data Analysis

Analyzed FCS flow cytometry data in R, combining plate-level quality checks, cell gating, and control-derived fluorescence thresholds to summarize YFP expression and caspase activation.

FCSInput data for individual cell events
2Fluorescence channels: YFP & active caspase
R / BioconductorflowCoreflowStatsggplot2
Project details
Question & analysis scope

How can plate-level quality checks and control-derived thresholds summarize single-cell signals for YFP expression and caspase activation? This academic project matched FCS files to plateIndex well annotations and examined a mock control and well 6, containing a CIDE protein with an N-terminal YFP tag.

Data preparation & quality checks

Read FCS files with flowCore, counted recorded events per well, and mapped them onto an A–H by 1–12 plate heatmap, retaining missing wells as missing values. Used an FSC-H versus SSC-H scatterplot to inspect the event distribution in the first well.

Gating method & sensitivity

Explored two-dimensional normal gating with norm2Filter and separately constructed elliptical gates from the mean, covariance, and Mahalanobis distance. Compared scales of 1, 1.5, and 2 to assess the tradeoff between retaining dense central events and including peripheral events. The report identified limitations in representing the cell population with a single two-dimensional normal distribution.

Control-derived thresholds & event fractions

Plotted mock-control FL1-H (YFP) and FL4-H (active caspase) signals as scatterplots and histograms. Set each channel's threshold to the control mean plus two standard deviations, then applied these cutoffs to well 6. Calculated YFP-positive events as a fraction of all events, and YFP/caspase double-positive events as a fraction of YFP-positive events, keeping the denominators distinct. This step used all recorded events in well 6 without applying the earlier scatter gate.

Output & interpretation

Developed an R analysis script for plate heatmaps, scatterplots, fluorescence histograms, gating overlays, and event-fraction summaries. Interpretation accounts for gate size and control-derived thresholds. The report recommends additional debris exclusion, singlet gating, and fluorescence checks to strengthen confidence in the identified populations.
